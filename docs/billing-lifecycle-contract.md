## 3. What the provider documentation says [DOC]

- [Create a price](https://developer.paddle.com/api-reference/prices/create-price/): a recurring price takes a billing cycle of interval `year`, frequency `1`; the unit amount is a string in the currency's minor unit, so `1999` for GBP or USD; `tax_mode` `internal` makes the amount tax-inclusive; quantity minimum and maximum can both be `1`; a null trial period keeps the provider out of the no-card app trial and beta. Actual price identifiers, the checkout total after local tax, and whether the seller account is enabled for these settings are [UNVERIFIED].
- [Cancel a subscription](https://developer.paddle.com/api-reference/subscriptions/cancel-subscription/): `effective_from` defaults to `next_billing_period` and records a scheduled change; `immediately` cancels at once. A canceled subscription cannot be reinstated. Past-due and paused subscriptions are handled differently. This contract therefore keeps the paid-through date in its own ledger and does not assume the provider's cancellation timing preserves the period.
- [Adjustments](https://developer.paddle.com/api-reference/adjustments/): refunds and chargebacks are adjustments against a transaction. Statuses are `pending_approval`, `approved`, `rejected` and `reversed`. Adjustments are immutable once created. The documentation says sandbox refunds are approved automatically about every ten minutes. A chargeback creates an adjustment; not every dispute warning is an actual refund.
- [How webhooks work](https://developer.paddle.com/webhooks/about/how-webhooks-work/): delivery is at least once, order is not guaranteed, and the endpoint should answer `200` within five seconds. The delivered wrapper carries `event_id`, `event_type`, `occurred_at`, a `notification_id` and the entity under `data`. Whether a redelivery of one event repeats `data` exactly is [UNVERIFIED] (E4).
- [Signature verification](https://developer.paddle.com/webhooks/about/signature-verification/): an HMAC-SHA-256 over a timestamp and the raw request body, compared securely. The official SDKs apply a five-second timestamp tolerance by default.
- [Webhook simulator](https://developer.paddle.com/webhooks/simulator/test-webhooks/): sends sample events. It does not create or mutate entities, so a simulated event has no matching subscription, transaction or adjustment to read back.
- [Subscriptions](https://developer.paddle.com/api-reference/subscriptions/): `management_urls` are authenticated customer portal links for updating payment and cancelling, returned only to a key holding the customer portal session write permission.
- [Payment recovery](https://developer.paddle.com/build/retain/configure-payment-recovery-dunning/): the provider can retry failed payments and message the customer. Whether it is enabled on this account, and its schedule, are [UNVERIFIED].
- [Sandbox email recipients](https://developer.paddle.com/changelog/2024/sandbox-emails-recipient-domain/) records an older domain restriction. The current [Sandbox guide](https://developer.paddle.com/sdks/sandbox/) describes forwarding non-matching-domain messages to the account email. This difference requires actual receipt checks in E7; a provider send status is not customer delivery evidence.

## 4. Environments, secrets and ownership [PROPOSED]

- **Two closed environments.** `sandbox` and `live` are separate Worker deployments with separate D1 databases, separate Paddle API keys, separate webhook endpoint secrets, separate client-side tokens, separate price identifiers and separate lease signing keys. A deployment is configured for exactly one environment and one pinned Paddle API host; every billing row also stores `env`, and a row whose `env` differs from the deployment refuses. A release build of the app trusts no sandbox lease key (licensing G6), so a sandbox purchase can never produce production access.
- **Secrets.** The Paddle API key and webhook secret exist only as Worker secrets. The Mac app holds no Paddle secret, calls no Paddle API and embeds no checkout. No key, signature header, management URL or portal token is written to a log, an audit event, a URL the service controls, or an operator view.
- **Payment data.** Card and payment-method entry happens only in the provider's checkout and customer portal. No route of this service accepts payment details from a browser or the app.
- **Ownership.** Ordinary household billing management derives account ownership from the verified session (licensing section 9). Initial app purchase uses a separate protected installation-bound intent/claim, and website purchase uses the compatible verified-email claim path in section 5 and [payment activation](payment-activation-contract.md); neither inherits household access from contact email. No billing route accepts an account, customer, subscription, transaction, price, quantity, currency or email from the request as authority. The email typed into checkout is not identity and is never used to find an account.
- **Configuration.** The price identifier per environment and currency is deployment configuration. No eligible household price identifier is configured here; the visible existing catalogue contains retired SaaS prices (section 13).

Reconciliation must confirm payment before consuming the claim and allocating exactly one slot/entitlement. **Existing verified-session path [PROPOSED, incomplete for initial purchase].** The following mechanics apply to an already authenticated household purchase only; they are not an implementation-ready anonymous/app or website purchase contract.

**Server-owned purchase intent for an already verified household.** Initial app-originated paid access and unbound website purchases instead use [payment activation P1-P3](payment-activation-contract.md), with restricted installation possession and pending-owner guards; the session/CSRF requirements here apply to this existing account-page route, not a pre-purchase email wall. `POST /v1/billing/purchase-intents` (session, CSRF and exact `Origin` on the web account page, client idempotency key) does the following:

1. Refuses when new sales are switched off (section 12), when the account already has a subscription in this environment that is not ended, or when an unexpired open intent exists. Single-open-intent and single-live-subscription are enforced by conditional SQL on the D1 primary, not by a read followed by a write.
2. Inserts an intent row: random 256-bit nonce (stored as a keyed HMAC), `account_id`, `env`, the configured `price_id`, `quantity = 1`, state `created`, a short expiry.
3. Creates the provider transaction server-side for exactly that price and quantity, then stores the returned transaction identifier on the intent with a conditional update, before anything is returned to the browser.
4. Returns only what the provider checkout needs to open that transaction.

A transaction the service did not create and store never maps to an account. If step 3's response is lost, the intent expires unused and the customer starts again; an orphan transaction has no checkout handle and no mapping. Creating a transaction by API and collecting it in the provider checkout as the start of an automatically renewing subscription is [UNVERIFIED] (E3).

**What the browser cannot do.** The success redirect, the checkout's client-side completion callback, `custom_data`, a transaction identifier in a query string and the checkout email are untrusted. `GET /v1/billing/purchase-intents/{id}` reports `processing`, `completed`, `failed` or `expired` for display and grants nothing. The account page says "Payment received, confirming" until the ledger says otherwise.

**Qualifying transaction.** A paid period is established only when reconciliation (section 7) has read the transaction from the provider with this environment's key and all of the following hold: it is the transaction stored on an intent of this account, or its subscription is already mapped to this account; status is `completed`; exactly one line with the configured price and quantity `1`; currency GBP or USD; it carries a billing period with `start < end`. The totals are recorded as evidence; an exact total rule waits for E2. A pending, ready, billed, past-due or cancelled transaction grants nothing, and unknown state grants nothing.

**Paid period.** Each qualifying transaction yields one immutable period row keyed by `(env, provider_transaction_id)`. Three checks are kept apart:

- **The transaction's own interval.** Start and end come from that completed transaction's billing period and are the period's original bounds for good. They are what a later refund, hold or audit refers to.
- **Association.** The first transaction maps to the account only through the server-owned intent. A later transaction qualifies only because it names a subscription already mapped to the account, with the configured price and quantity.
- **Current period, where applicable.** Only the subscription's most recent completed billing transaction is compared with the subscription's current billing period from the same run; a mismatch there is a contradictory read (section 7), not a grant. An older transaction's interval is not expected to equal the current period after a renewal and is never rejected or rewritten for that reason. It stays a valid, auditable and refundable period.

**`paidThrough`.** Take the union of the original intervals of the account's periods in this environment, leaving out every period ended as refunded or charged back, except that a period with a recorded restoration (section 8, D3) contributes only from the restoration time to its original end. The paid source grants when now falls inside that union, and `paidThrough` is the end of the unbroken run containing now. Independent periods, such as those of an ended subscription and of a later one, all count and none is lost; removing one refunded or charged-back period leaves any unrelated period that covers now still granting. `paidThrough` is never extended by a future scheduled renewal date, a next-billing date, an unpaid or previewed transaction, a webhook payload or the client.

## 6. Webhook intake [PROPOSED]

Route `POST /v1/provider/paddle/webhook`, unauthenticated except by signature, one per environment deployment. Order of work:

1. Bound the body (proposed 256 KiB; the largest real payload is E9) and read the exact raw bytes once. The stored body is never re-serialised, and only these raw bytes are ever checked against the HMAC.
2. Parse the signature header, bound its timestamp against the service clock (proposed: the documented five seconds, with a small fixed allowance for future skew), compute the HMAC over the documented timestamp-and-raw-body construction with each configured secret for this environment, and compare in constant time. The exact header grammar is pinned from the documentation page and a captured sandbox delivery in slice B1. Whether a provider retry carries a fresh timestamp decides whether five seconds is workable and is E8.
3. On failure: answer non-2xx, store nothing but a bounded counter. An unverified body never reaches the inbox.
4. Only after the signature is valid, parse strictly. A duplicate object key at any depth is detected by the parser itself, before any value is used, and refuses; so do invalid UTF-8, unpaired surrogates, non-finite numbers, numbers outside the I-JSON exactly representable range, and input beyond fixed depth, member and string bounds. The number domain actually allowed per field is pinned from the real event schema against captured fixtures (E4). Input that cannot be represented unambiguously is never repaired: its raw delivery record is kept, a quarantine record and operator alert are written, no projection and no reconciliation mark is made, and the answer is non-2xx. No field of `data` is ever dropped to make input fit.
5. Build the **event projection**: exactly `event_id`, `event_type`, `occurred_at` and the whole `data` object, excluding `notification_id` and every other delivery detail. Its canonical form is the JSON Canonicalization Scheme of [RFC 8785](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8785) (deterministic member ordering and number and string serialisation over I-JSON input), and the semantic event SHA-256 is taken over those canonical bytes. The canonical form is derived data for comparison only; it is never signature-checked and never replaces the raw body.
6. In one D1 `batch()`: insert a **raw delivery record** for this receive attempt (attempt identifier, `notification_id`, raw-body SHA-256, signature timestamp, receive time; distinct raw bodies are stored once each by that SHA-256); insert the event projection keyed by `(env, event_id)` with its semantic SHA-256, doing nothing on conflict; and mark a subject as needing reconciliation only if this attempt is the one that inserted the projection.
7. Outcomes. A projection that already existed with the same semantic SHA-256 is an accepted duplicate whatever its `notification_id`, whitespace or key order: answer `200`, no further effect. One with a different semantic SHA-256 is a conflict: the original projection is never overwritten, the conflicting delivery is kept as a raw record, a conflict record and operator alert are written, no reconciliation mark is made from it, and the answer is non-2xx (proposed `409`).
8. Answer `200` only after the durable write committed. A failed write answers `500` so the provider retries. No provider call and no entitlement work happens before the acknowledgement.

Both levels are append-only: every verified delivery is preserved for audit, and no body, hash or projection is updated or deleted in this scope. The replay defence is the unique `(env, event_id)` projection, not the timestamp window.

**Subject hint.** For a known event type, a bounded subject identifier (subscription, transaction or adjustment) is read from `data` solely to choose what to reconcile. It is a hint, not ledger authority: it can only cause a re-read of a subject that section 5 already maps, and it attaches no owner. An unknown event type, or a subject that maps nowhere, is stored and then ignored or quarantined (section 7). The payload, its `occurred_at` and arrival order are never the source of entitlement state. The full `data` schema per event type, and its stability between deliveries of one event, are captured from the sandbox in E4 before B1 is accepted.

## 7. Reconciliation [PROPOSED]

**One writer per subject.** Each subscription (and each not-yet-subscribed intent transaction) has a reconcile row with `wanted` and `done` generations and a fenced claim. Intake and the periodic sweep only increment `wanted`. A worker claims by conditional update, runs, and commits only while its fence token is still current. If `wanted` moved during the run the subject stays dirty and runs again, so a burst, a duplicate or a backlog collapses into repeated current-state reads.

**Read current state, then decide.** A run fetches from the provider, with this environment's key and host: the subscription, its transactions, and the adjustments on those transactions. It verifies the mapping to an account (section 5), then calls a pure function `derive(readSet, ledgerRows, now)` that returns the ledger changes. Nothing is derived from the triggering event's body.

**Provider reads are not a snapshot.** Several GETs are not transaction-consistent with each other. Each run records a read generation, the service-clock window of its reads and each entity's provider update time, and commits only if the read set is internally consistent: every transaction names the subscription, every adjustment names a transaction in the set and its refunded lines and totals map onto that transaction, and the latest completed billing transaction agrees with the subscription's current period (section 5). A contradictory read set commits nothing and retries with bounded backoff; if it persists the subject is held and alerted. It is never recorded as a coherent remote state. This contract cannot promise that a provider read is never stale: provider reads are eventually fresh, bounded in practice by the next trigger, the sweep and the provider's read rate limits, which nobody has checked (section 13 A).

**Atomic commit on the D1 primary.** Each run generates a unique random `commit_nonce`, bound to its fence token and never reused. One `batch()`:

1. The first statement is a single compare-and-swap on the shared `account_guard` row owned by licensing migration 0001: it requires `account_revision = :read`, the subject's subscription `revision = :read` and this run's fence token still current, and it sets `account_revision + 1` and `commit_nonce = :nonce`. The new revision and the nonce are written only by this statement succeeding.
2. Every following statement is conditioned on the guard row holding exactly this run's `commit_nonce`, not on a revision number existing. That covers the subscription revision, period, grace and adjustment rows, the `entitlementRevision` increment, audit, outbox, and the reconcile row's `done` generation. A run whose swap matched nothing therefore writes nothing, even when another run already owns the revision it was aiming for.
3. Whether access changed, and so whether `entitlementRevision` moves, is computed by fresh reads inside those same statements, not from values carried through JavaScript, so source precedence and a concurrent refund are decided on committed rows.

If any statement errors the whole batch rolls back [licensing section 9, DOC]. If the first statement changes zero rows, every following statement changes zero rows; the worker reads that count, marks nothing done or clean, and reruns from a fresh read. The account revision serialises all of an account's subscriptions and sources, not one subject: any writer that changes a source for the account (trial, beta, revocation, another subscription) must increment the same account revision in its own batch. Licensing section 9 now carries this same guard, including email/provider mappings and activation/support writers. There is one account guard; attempt nonces fence every dependent write. No JavaScript read-modify-write transaction is assumed. Conditioned multi-statement behaviour on remote D1 is unproven and is part of PAT-2592 and E12.

**Monotonic facts.** A period row is inserted once and never re-dated. A grace row is inserted once per `(env, subscription, anchor paidThrough)`. A period's `ended` reason is set once; a D3 restoration is a separate, audited, append-only row that references the end and never edits it or re-dates the period. Each stored provider-derived field keeps the provider update time and read generation it came from, and a write carrying an older one is refused by its own condition, so an older provider read cannot overwrite newer facts, re-open, extend or re-date anything. A stale read that omits a newer transaction delays a grant and never removes one.

**Outage.** If the provider cannot be read, nothing is committed, the subject stays dirty with bounded backoff, and the ledger keeps what it had. Disclosed consequence: if the provider is unreachable across a renewal, the old period ends at `paidThrough`, no grace exists because no failure was confirmed, and paid access resumes only when the completed renewal is read. This is not the licence-service outage allowance and does not borrow from it.

**Sweep.** Proposed: every subscription that is not ended is reconciled at least once every 24 hours, and all of them after any intake outage, so a missed webhook is repaired without relying on provider retries. The trigger mechanism (scheduled Worker or queue) does not exist and is not a verified capability. It needs the service placement and toolchain of licensing G4 and binding authority under PAT-2592; licensing G7 as written does not cover a scheduled trigger (R6).

**Unmapped.** An event or entity that maps to no intent and no known subscription, or to a different account than expected, is quarantined: recorded, alerted, granting nothing, and never attached to an account automatically.

## 8. Lifecycle to ledger

The lease states and evidence objects are those of licensing sections 3 to 5. This section defines only the paid and grace sources.

| Ledger condition for the account, in this environment | Paid source contributes | Customer status shown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No qualifying period ever | nothing | No subscription; purchase `processing` or `failed` if an intent is open |
| A period covers now | `paid` with `paidThrough` | Active, renews on date; or Cancelled, access ends on date |
| Latest period ended at `paidThrough`, grace row exists for it, `now < graceDeadline`, grace not voided | `grace` with `paidThrough`, `graceDeadline` | Payment failed, update payment, access ends on date |
| Latest period ended, no grace or grace over | nothing | Ended; payment failed if applicable |
| Latest period ended by confirmed full refund | nothing | Refunded |
| Latest period ended by provider-confirmed chargeback, no restoration | nothing; no grace | Ended; payment reversed by the card issuer (wording is R2) |
| Chargeback period with a reviewed D3 restoration and `now` before its original end | `paid` for the remaining original period only, `paidThrough` = original end | Active until the original date; not renewing |
| Partial refund recorded, or dispute or warning open, on a period that covers now | unchanged: `paid` with the same `paidThrough` | Unchanged for the customer; flagged for operator review |
| Grace row exists and voluntary cancellation is confirmed | nothing: grace voided; the paid-through date has already passed | Cancelled, access ended; shown after the customer confirmed knowing grace would end |

**Source precedence [PROPOSED, licensing G5].** Account and activation revocation override everything. Otherwise the lease `state` is the first granting source in the order `paid`, `grace`, `beta`, `trial`; with none granting it is `expired` if a paid, trial or beta source once granted and `none` otherwise. Ending the paid source never ends a beta invitation or an unexpired trial, and the reverse [OWNER B5]. A refunded or charged-back paid source is not an account revocation: the lease becomes whatever remains, with a higher `entitlementRevision` and `sequence`, which under licensing section 6 replaces the cache and suppresses the older paid lease.

**Renewal.** A completed renewal transaction on a mapped subscription is a new qualifying period. Nothing extends `paidThrough` before that transaction is read as completed.

**Voluntary cancellation [OWNER B4].** `POST /v1/billing/subscription/cancel` asks the provider to cancel at the next billing period, then confirms by reading the subscription back. States: `none`, `requested` (not yet confirmed, shown as "Cancellation not yet confirmed"), `scheduled` (provider shows the scheduled cancellation and its date), `effective`. A timeout or ambiguous response stays `requested` and is reconciled; it is never shown as complete. Access to `paidThrough` comes from the service's own period row, so it holds whatever the provider's status later becomes. Because a canceled subscription cannot be reinstated [DOC], buying again creates a new intent, a new subscription and new, separate periods. Removing a scheduled cancellation before it takes effect is not offered until E5 shows how the provider supports it. **During failed-payment grace [OWNER D4]:** while a grace row is active, the cancel route refuses unless the request carries an explicit acknowledgement that grace access will end, and the web and app screens show that explanation before the customer confirms. The acknowledgement is recorded in the audit event. A `requested` or otherwise unconfirmed cancellation does not void grace and is never shown as complete; grace is voided only once the read-back shows the cancellation `scheduled` or `effective`. The void is a one-way, audited ledger entry on the grace row; the safe expiry of B8 then applies (no new updates, viewing, settings and history stay, an in-flight update finishes or is recovered, cached allowances unchanged and never renewed once the revocation is known).

**Failed renewal and grace [OWNER B7].** The service records grace only when a reconciliation read shows the subscription past due with an uncompleted renewal for the period starting at the current `paidThrough`, the subscription has no requested or scheduled cancellation, and the anchoring period is not refunded or charged back. The exact provider fields that prove a failed renewal are E6. `graceDeadline = paidThrough + 604,800` by checked integer arithmetic. The insert is conditional on the unique anchor, so retries, duplicates, delays and repeated failures change nothing. Grace ends early only by a completed renewal (a new period, normal `paid`), or by confirmed cancellation (D4 above), full refund or confirmed chargeback of the subscription. An open dispute alone does not end grace. Provider recovery may continue after the deadline; a later completed payment restores `paid` for the period the provider reports, which is recorded as E6 evidence rather than assumed. The client never infers grace (licensing O6).

**Full refund [OWNER B5].** An adjustment is a full refund only when reconciliation reads it as an approved refund covering the whole of one qualifying transaction. `pending_approval` and `rejected` leave access unchanged. On an approved full refund: that transaction's period is ended with reason `refunded`, `entitlementRevision` increments, and other periods and sources are untouched. Separately, the service uses the shared renewal-stop record below and requests cancellation of the subscription, tracking `renewalStop` as `requested` then `confirmed` only from a read-back. A refunded period with `renewalStop` not confirmed is an operator alert, not a completed outcome. Creating a refund is a provider mutation with its own authority (R5); eligibility under the 30-day offer is not automated here.

**Partial refund [OWNER D1].** An approved adjustment that does not cover the whole of one qualifying transaction is a partial refund. The ledger records the adjustment with its amount and currency and flags it for operator/support review (PAT-2590). It changes no period, `paidThrough`, renewal, grace or lease, creates no hold and never stops renewal. An operator's review mark is audit only and also changes no access. Pending and rejected partial adjustments likewise change nothing.

**Open dispute or warning [OWNER D2].** A dispute or chargeback warning, or an open dispute, puts the affected period into `hold`: a review flag for reconciliation and operator review. A hold never ends, shortens or re-dates the period. The paid source keeps granting and paid leases keep being renewed up to that period's original end and never beyond it, and renewal is not stopped. A hold establishes no new entitlement from the affected transaction [PROPOSED, conservative]: no grace is anchored on it and no purchase intent is accepted while it is open. Other periods and sources are unaffected. A hold is not an operator's to clear at will. It leaves when reconciliation reads the dispute resolved in the customer's favour (cleared with its evidence, access never having changed), or when it becomes a confirmed chargeback. A correction outside those needs a recorded policy (PAT-2590) or its own separate authorisation. No hold exit grants arbitrary time or resets a trial.

**Confirmed chargeback [OWNER D2].** A chargeback is confirmed only when reconciliation reads a provider adjustment of chargeback type in a confirmed final status (exact type and status values are E10; none is assumed) covering the whole of one qualifying transaction. Then, in one commit: that transaction's period is ended with reason `charged_back`, no grace row is created or kept for it, `entitlementRevision` increments, and unrelated periods and sources are untouched. Separately the service requests cancellation of the subscription and tracks `renewalStop` as `requested` then `confirmed` only from a provider read-back, independently of the entitlement change; the chargeback is never reported complete while `renewalStop` is unconfirmed, and an unconfirmed `renewalStop` is an operator alert. The safe expiry of B8 applies when the app receives the revocation. A chargeback adjustment that cannot be mapped onto exactly one qualifying transaction is held for operator review with access unchanged and is part of the E10 evidence.

**Reversed chargeback [OWNER D3].** A reversal is acted on only after an operator (PAT-2590) has reviewed the provider-confirmed reversal, read back by reconciliation, and recorded the review. The restoration is an append-only row restoring only the span from the restoration time to the period's original end, and only if that original end is still after the restoration time. The original end, `paidThrough` and the period row are not changed or extended, no compensation time is added, and time lost between the chargeback and the restoration is not back-paid. If the original end has passed, nothing is restored and the case is recorded. A restoration does not restart renewal: `renewalStop` stays as it was and the subscription is not reinstated, since a canceled subscription cannot be reinstated [DOC]; continued service after the original end needs a new purchase. A restoration raises `entitlementRevision` and `sequence` so the new lease replaces the revoked state. A reversed *refund* is not covered by D3: it is recorded and held for operator review with no automatic access change until the owner decides.

**Shared renewal-stop single flight [PROPOSED].** Migration 0003 owns `billing_renewal_stop`, uniquely keyed `(env, subscription_id, renewal_generation)`. Customer cancel, support cancellation/refund and chargeback reconciliation all join that same record under the shared account guard; causes are append-only references, not new dispatches. States: `awaitingRefundApproval`, `intent`, `dispatching`, `unknown`, `accepted`, `confirmed`, `notRequired`, `needsOperator`. Refund preparation can reserve `awaitingRefundApproval`; only approved full refund enables `intent`. A rejected refund closes its own cause, never another independent customer-cancel/chargeback cause. Provider read-back showing scheduled/effective cancellation confirms the shared row without another call. Dispatch atomically moves `intent` once before network I/O; ambiguous or lost results are read back only, never blindly retried. A fence cannot recall an in-flight request. New generation is possible only after an independently evidenced renewal-state change/new subscription and fresh authorised intent; reversal never creates it. Overall refund/chargeback completion requires its adjustment outcome AND confirmed renewal stop. These records are financial/reconciliation state, not 30-day ordinary API retries.

## 9. Customer status and provider links [PROPOSED]

`GET /v1/licence` (licensing section 9) gains a display-only `subscription` object: `status` (`none`, `active`, `cancelling`, `pastDue`, `ended`), `paidThrough`, `renewsAt` (present only when the subscription will renew), `cancellation` (`state`, `accessEnds`), `payment` (`state`, `graceDeadline`), `refund` (`none`, `pending`, `refunded`), `endReason` (present only when `ended`: `expired`, `refunded`, `chargeback`) and `purchase` (`none`, `processing`, `failed`). A partial refund, an open dispute and a hold change none of these values for the customer; they are visible to operators only (D1, D2). Before a customer confirms cancellation during grace, the account page and app show that grace access will end (D4). It grants nothing; only a verified lease does. The app shows these values and opens the web account page in the system browser for buying, cancelling and updating payment.

`POST /v1/billing/portal-session` (session, CSRF) returns a redirect to the provider's authenticated management link for the subscription mapped to the session's account [DOC]. Links are fetched on demand, never stored, never logged, sent with `Cache-Control: no-store` and `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, and never placed in a page that loads third-party resources. The API key permission this needs is R4.

## 10. Renewal reminders [OWNER B6, mechanism PROPOSED]

- **Ownership.** Patched schedules the 30-day and 7-day notices from the reconciled provider state. Whether the provider also sends an annual renewal notice, when, and whether it can be configured is E7. Until E7 is evidenced no notice is suppressed on the assumption that the other party sent an equivalent, and a provider or statutory notice that is mandatory is never cancelled as a duplicate.
- **Schedule.** A notice row is keyed by `(env, subscription, renewal date, price, currency, notice type, generation)`. `generation` increments whenever renewal date, price or cancellation state changes; the same batch voids pending rows of older generations. A subscription with a requested or scheduled cancellation, a refunded or charged-back period, a stopped renewal, or grace has no pending renewal notice. A partial refund or an open dispute alone leaves renewal unchanged (D1, D2), so it voids no notice.
- **Send.** One atomic send claim: a single conditional statement claims a due `pending` row only while its generation equals the subscription's current generation, and the same batch writes the audit record with the decision time and generation. The batch that changes the generation voids unsent rows, so a claim on an older generation matches nothing. The adapter then gets a stable message identifier derived from the row key. The recipient is the account's verified email on record, never an address from a provider payload or a request. A pending unverified purchase contact is not eligible for this reminder path; resolve ownership/contact verification and observed provider notice responsibilities under R8/E7 before live sales.
- **Race disclosed.** No transaction spans D1 and an external message. Claims and state changes are ordered on the D1 primary: a claim committed before the change that would have voided it may still be handed to the transport and arrive afterwards, and a cancellation made at the provider is unknown here until reconciled. So this contract does not promise that no reminder is ever received after a cancellation. It promises that none is claimed after the ledger recorded the change, and that the audit shows each notice was valid when claimed.
- **Delivery truth.** States are `pending`, `claimed`, `accepted` (the transport accepted it), `ambiguous`, `failed`, `voided`, `missed`. `accepted` is not `delivered` and is never reported as such. A lost acknowledgement is `ambiguous`: it is held, checked by message identifier where the transport supports read-back, and otherwise left for an operator rather than resent blind. That hold is delivery handling only; it is not a notification policy and does not alter B6. Exactly-once delivery is not claimed.
- **Late windows.** A 30-day notice not sent before the 7-day point is recorded `missed`; a 7-day notice not sent before the charge is recorded `missed`. Neither is sent after its meaning has passed.
- **Authority.** Sending any email, the notice wording and the transport binding are R3 and PAT-2592. Nothing was sent by this run.

**Rollback.** A new-sales switch refuses new purchase intents only.
